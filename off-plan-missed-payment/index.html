Missed an off-plan payment? Dubai law gives you 30 days

Good news for off-plan buyers: in Dubai, a developer can't cancel your contract the moment a payment is late. The law makes it go through Dubai Land Department first, and you get a 30-day notice to pay. If the payment is still missing after that, what the developer may do depends on how far construction is. Here is the rule in plain English, with a calculator.

  • DLD steps in first: the developer must notify Dubai Land Department, which gives you 30 days' notice. Law 9/2009 ›
  • 80%+ built: the developer may keep the contract and claim the balance, or ask for a public auction. Art. 11 ›
  • 60% to 80% built: it may cancel and keep up to 40% of the unit value in the contract.
  • Started, below 60%: it may cancel and keep up to 25% of the unit value.
  • Not started (for reasons beyond the developer's control): up to 30% of what you paid.

Source: Dubai Law No. 13 of 2008, Article 11, as amended by Law No. 9 of 2009 · dlp.dubai.gov.ae · last checked 26 Nov 2026

What the reel saidThe short version, in 30 seconds

In Dubai, one late off-plan payment doesn't cancel your deal overnight. The developer has to go through Dubai Land Department, which gives you a 30-day notice to pay. If it's still unpaid, the outcome depends on the build: at 80% or more the developer keeps the deal and claims the rest; from 60% it can cancel and keep up to 40% of the price; below 60%, up to 25%; and if work hasn't started through no fault of the developer, up to 30% of what you paid. The smart move: plan your cash for every instalment, not just the booking.

The rule in one tableArticle 11, Dubai Law No. 13 of 2008, as amended by Law No. 9 of 2009

Construction stageWhat the developer may do after the 30-day notice

"Construction works" means the developer has taken possession of the site and started building under plans approved by the competent authorities. In the 60%+ and below-60% cases, anything due back to you must be paid within one year of the termination date, or within 60 days of the unit being sold, whichever is earlier.

What happens, step by step

  1. A payment is missed. The developer can't cancel on its own. It has to notify Dubai Land Department of the breach.
  2. DLD gives you 30 days' notice to meet your contract obligations, delivered in person, by registered mail or by email. Use this time: pay, or talk to the developer about a solution in writing.
  3. If you pay within the 30 days, the contract carries on.
  4. If not, the developer's options depend on the construction stage in the table above. The percentages are maximums, not fixed penalties.
  5. Money back: where the contract is cancelled at 60%+ or below 60%, the balance due to you is paid within one year of termination, or within 60 days of the unit being resold, whichever comes first.
  6. If you keep paying to the end, once the project gets its completion certificate the developer must register completed units in the Property Register in the names of buyers who met their obligations: your Oqood (interim registration) becomes your title deed.

What it means for off-plan buyers

  • Your sale must be registered. Under Law 13/2008, an off-plan sale not entered in DLD's Interim Property Register (Oqood) is void. Check yours is registered.
  • Pay into the project's escrow account. If RERA cancels a project, the developer must return all payments under Law No. 8 of 2007 on escrow accounts.
  • Later stages carry more weight. Once a building passes 80%, the developer can hold you to the full price, so the handover payment matters most.
  • Read your SPA. Your Sale and Purchase Agreement sets the instalment dates. Diarise every one.

A simple exampleIllustration only, not an offer or a price

Say your contract value is AED 1,500,000 and you've paid AED 450,000. The building is 65% complete and you miss an instalment. DLD sends you a 30-day notice. If you still can't pay and the developer cancels, it may keep at most 40% of AED 1,500,000 = AED 600,000. Because that's more than you've paid, the law's cap doesn't leave anything to return in this example. The same miss at 40% complete caps the deduction at 25% = AED 375,000, so at least AED 75,000 comes back to you. Planning your cash for every instalment protects you far more than any rule.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Budgeting only for the booking and the DLD fee, not for the instalments during construction and at handover.
  • Ignoring a DLD notice. The 30 days start from the notice: open every email and registered letter.
  • Assuming a bank will fund a missed instalment. On off-plan, UAE banks can lend at most 50% of the value. See the mortgage guide.
  • Agreeing a payment extension by phone only. Get any change in writing from the developer.
  • Buying in a project that isn't registered with DLD or doesn't use an escrow account.

Missed payment questions

Can a Dubai developer cancel my off-plan contract the day after I miss a payment?

No. Under Article 11 of Dubai Law No. 13 of 2008 (as amended by Law No. 9 of 2009), the developer must notify Dubai Land Department, and the Department gives you 30 days' notice to meet your obligations before the developer can use any of the remedies.

How do I receive the 30-day notice?

The law says the Department notifies the purchaser in person, by registered mail or by email. Keep your contact details up to date with your developer and DLD.

How much can the developer keep if the contract is cancelled?

It depends on completion: from 60% built it may keep up to 40% of the unit value in the contract; below 60% up to 25%; if work has not started for reasons beyond its control, up to 30% of the payments made. These are maximums.

What if the project is 80% or more complete?

The developer may keep the contract, retain what you paid and claim the rest of the price. If that fails, it may ask for the unit to be sold at public auction to recover what is owed.

When is the rest of my money returned?

In the 60%+ and below-60% cases, within one year of the termination date or within 60 days of the unit being sold, whichever is earlier.

What if the project itself is cancelled?

If RERA cancels a project, the developer must return all amounts paid, under Law No. 8 of 2007 on escrow accounts.
