One number decides your real yield in Dubai

Good news for buyers: in Dubai, the yearly service charge on an apartment isn't a guess. The law makes RERA approve the budget before anyone collects a dirham, your share is worked out from your area, and the approved rate is free to check on Dubai Land Department's Service Charge Index. Here's how it works, with a calculator for your yield after charges.

What the reel saidThe short version, in 30 seconds

Before you buy in Dubai, check the service charge: it decides what the apartment really earns you. Three rules. One: under Law 6 of 2019 it pays to run, maintain and repair the common parts. Two: RERA must approve the budget before anyone collects a dirham. Three, the one that surprises most buyers: your share is simply your unit's area divided by the building's total area. So you can check it for free on DLD's Service Charge Index: approved rate, times your area. And because unpaid charges block a sale of the unit, put them into your yield from day one.

How to check the rate, step by stepFree, on DLD's website or the Dubai REST app

  1. Open the Service Charge Index in DLD e-services, or open the Dubai REST app and choose Services.
  2. Pick the master project, the project, the usage (for example residential) and the budget year, then press Calculate.
  3. You'll see the RERA-approved rate for that project and year.
  4. Multiply the rate by your unit's area for an estimate of your yearly share. The index notes that its figure doesn't include arrears.
  5. Put it in the calculator above with your price and rent to see your yield after service charges.
  6. Once you own the unit, payment instructions come from Mollak, DLD's system for service charges, and you pay into RERA-approved accounts.

What they areAnnual charges collected from owners to cover managing, operating, maintaining and repairing the common parts (Art. 2).
Who must payEach owner pays the management entity his share of the annual service charges (Art. 25). Renting the unit out doesn't move this to the tenant (Art. 16), and an owner can't refuse to pay RERA-approved charges (Art. 28).

Your shareBased on the ratio of your unit's area to the total area of the jointly owned property, as recorded in the Real Property Register (Art. 25).
Unsold unitsThe developer pays its share for unsold units (Art. 25).

What it means for off-plan buyers

  • Compare yields after charges, not before. Two projects with the same rent can leave you with very different income once the approved service charge is taken off.
  • New projects may not have a rate yet. If the index shows nothing for a building under construction, ask the developer in writing what its estimate is based on, and treat it as an estimate.
  • Read your SPA for the start date. The law makes the developer pay for unsold units, and your contract can say who covers charges for a sold unit and from when. Check that clause before you sign.
  • Amenities cost money every year. Pools, gyms, concierge and landscaping are lovely, and they're part of the common parts the charges pay for.
  • Selling later? Clear any service charges first: the unit can't be transferred until they're paid.

A simple exampleA formula, not a rate for any project

Say you're looking at a unit of A square feet, and the Service Charge Index shows an approved rate of R AED per square foot for that building this year. Your yearly service charge is about A × R, and per month about A × R ÷ 12. If the unit costs P and rents for Y a year, your gross yield is Y ÷ P, and your yield after service charges is (Y − A × R) ÷ P. Put the real numbers in the calculator above and compare two or three projects side by side before you choose.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Comparing projects on rent alone, without checking each one's approved service charge.
  • Using a brochure figure instead of the RERA-approved rate on the DLD index.
  • Leaving service charges out of your cash plan for the first year after handover.
  • Assuming your tenant pays them. The law keeps the owner liable even when the unit is leased.
  • Letting charges build up, then finding the unit can't be sold until they're cleared.

Service charge questions

What are service charges in Dubai?

Under Dubai Law No. 6 of 2019, service charges are the annual charges collected from owners to cover the cost of managing, operating, maintaining and repairing the common parts of a jointly owned building or community.

Who approves service charges?

RERA. The law says the management entity may not charge or collect any amount without RERA's approval, and RERA may not approve the budget unless a certified audit firm has approved it (Article 27).

How is my share worked out?

By area: your share is based on the ratio of your unit's area to the total area of the jointly owned property, using the area recorded in the Real Property Register (Article 25).

Where can I check the rate before I buy?

On the Dubai Land Department's Service Charge Index (DLD e-services) or in the Dubai REST app. Choose the master project, project, usage and year to see the RERA-approved rate, then multiply it by your unit's area.

Who pays service charges on unsold units?

The developer pays its share of the annual service charges for unsold units (Article 25).

What if an owner doesn't pay?

The developer or management may not stop an owner from using or taking possession of the unit to force payment (Article 29). But the management entity has a lien on the unit: it may not be sold or transferred until the charges are paid, the claim can be enforced through the Rental Disputes Center, and the unit may be sold at public auction (Article 32).
